The premise

Apple needs chips, and your company makes them. So do your rivals. Every round, Apple has a fixed amount it will buy, and it buys from the sellers it likes best first. Whoever is left at the end of the queue may sell nothing at all.

You control three things: how much you charge, how many chips you offer, and what you invest in. Everyone sees everyone else's price and quantity change live, as they type. That is where the bluffing, undercutting and deal-making happens.

After six rounds, the company with the highest Company Value wins.

How a round works

Every round follows the same order. The default timer is three minutes; the round ends early once every team has locked in.

  1. Your factory produces chipsEvery company makes 100 chips for free, plus 50 more for each Capacity level it owned when the round started. They are added to whatever you didn't sell last time.
  2. The news hitsOne Market Shock is announced. It changes a rule for this round only, for example Apple buys fewer chips, or Tech counts for more.
  3. Invest, if you want toBuy Tech or Capacity upgrades. Changed your mind? Undo any purchase from this round for a full refund, as long as you haven't locked in.
  4. Set your price and quantity, liveYour numbers appear on every team's order board the moment you type them. Your ticket shows your predicted finishing position, updated on every keystroke.
  5. Lock inLocking in makes your numbers final. If the timer runs out first, whatever is in your boxes is submitted for you.
  6. Apple buysApple ranks every company by Competitive Score and buys from the top down until its demand for the round is used up.
  7. ResultsRevenue lands in your cash, unsold chips stay in stock, and a new Market Price is set for next round.

How Apple chooses

Apple scores every company out of 100 on three things, then combines them with weights the host chooses. The defaults are Price 50%, Tech 30%, Capacity 20%.

Price score    = (highest − yours) ÷ (highest − lowest) × 100
  cheapest company gets 100, most expensive gets 0. If everyone charges the same, everyone gets 100.
Tech score     = Tech level × 20
Capacity score = Capacity level × 20

Competitive Score = Price score × 0.5 + Tech score × 0.3 + Capacity score × 0.2

One shared pool, not a quota

Apple's total demand is demand per team × number of teams. With the default of 70 per team and 5 teams, Apple buys 350 chips in total. That pool is filled from the highest score down. Ties go to whoever locked in first.

Because every company makes at least 100 chips a round, supply usually beats demand, so the bottom of the ranking gets squeezed. With five companies each offering 100 chips:

When total supply is below demand, everyone who offers chips sells all of them, whatever their rank. That is the moment a high price pays off, and it lasts only until a rival offers more.

Money, chips and upgrades

Every company starts with $5,000 (the host can change this). Cash can go negative: you're allowed to borrow to invest, and debt simply counts against you at the end.

Upgrade costs

Each upgrade costs 1.5× the previous one of the same kind. Both max out at level 5.

Tech raises your score immediately, so it helps this round. Capacity raises your score immediately too, and from next round on you produce 50 extra chips per level. Some Market Shocks and the host's inflation setting can make upgrades cheaper or dearer for a round.

Price cap and Market Price

The Market Price is the average price Apple actually paid last round, weighted by how many chips each company sold. Your price can't go above 3× the Market Price ($150 in round 1). Premium pricing is allowed; absurd numbers are not.

Unsold chips

Chips you don't sell stay in stock and can be offered again next round. At the end of the match each one is worth $10.

Winning

Company Value = cash + unsold chips × $10 + Tech level × $500 + Capacity level × $500

The highest Company Value after round 6 wins. Notice what the formula implies: an upgrade that cost $4,500 only stays on your books as $500. Every upgrade has to earn the difference back in sales before the match ends, so a late upgrade is much harder to justify than an early one.

Market shocks

Each round opens with one news event that bends the rules for that round only. Shocks exist so that no single strategy wins every match. The strength of each one is rolled fresh every time, and the host can turn overall volatility up or down. If the server has an AI key configured, shocks are written live and react to whatever strategy is dominating the room.

Playing as a team

Up to four people can run one company. When you create a team you give it a name and give yourself a name; teammates join later with the same room code and pick your team from the list.

  • Shared control. Anyone on the team can change the price and quantity, buy or undo upgrades, and press Lock In. Talk before you click.
  • Team channel. A private chat only your teammates can read.
  • All Teams channel. Every team reads it, but only each team's leader can post. The leader is the earliest teammate who is still connected, and it hands over automatically if they drop out.
  • Activity feed. Every lock-in and upgrade is announced in the All Teams channel with the team, and the person, who did it.

Contribution

Each action is credited to the person who actually took it, so a team, and a teacher, can see who drove each decision. Points come from decisions, not from clicking a lot: editing and chatting earn a little, with a cap every round.

The Analyst

Every screen has an Analyst button. It is an assistant that runs entirely inside your browser: no account, no AI service, nothing sent anywhere. It uses the exact formula the game server uses, fed with the numbers your team can already see.

  • Brief me: where you'd finish right now and the one or two changes that matter most.
  • Best price: simulates every price up to the cap with rivals' numbers held fixed and shows where your revenue peaks, plus the highest price that still sells everything you offered.
  • Buy Tech? / Buy Capacity?: what the next level does to your rank, sales and Company Value this round.
  • Rivals, this shock, rules: who is ahead and by how much, what the round's news means for you, and any rule in this guide.

You can ask in English or Korean. Its numbers are exact for the moment you ask, and they change as soon as a rival changes theirs.

Strategy

There is no single right build. Three broad paths, and what each one is exposed to:

Price leader

Stay cheap, invest little, sell in volume. Keeps cash high.

Hurt by: Budget Slack, a rival undercutting you, low demand rounds
Tech premium

Buy Tech early, charge more, rely on score from quality.

Hurt by: Talent War, Quality Scandal, upgrades that never pay back
Capacity scale

Build factories, flood the market in later rounds.

Hurt by: Supply Chain Crisis, Chip Glut, unsold stock at $10

Things good teams notice

  • Compare total supply on the order board with Apple's demand before you set quantity. If supply is short, everyone sells: raise your price.
  • Your price score depends on the spread between the cheapest and dearest company. One rival going very high makes everyone else look cheaper.
  • Upgrades only keep $500 of value. Round 5 and 6 upgrades rarely pay for themselves.
  • Locking in early wins ties, but shows your hand.

Hosting a match

  1. Open the host console and press Open market. You get a five-character room code.
  2. Press Projector view and put that tab on the big screen. Before the match it shows the code and the companies as they list; during the match it shows every company's value as candles, the leaderboard and the activity feed. It never shows cash.
  3. Players join on their own devices. You need at least 3 teams; fill gaps with bots (Tech, Capacity or Balanced style).
  4. Adjust settings if you like, then Ring the opening bell.
  5. During rounds you can pause, resume, end a round early, remove a team, and change demand, timer and volatility live.
  6. After round 6, download the match data and the contribution report as CSV, or reset the room to play again.

FAQ

Can I join after the match has started?

Yes, as a teammate on an existing team that has a free seat. New teams can only be created before round 1.

What happens if I close the tab or lose Wi-Fi?

Open the game again on the same device and you are put back in your seat automatically. Your team keeps playing while you're away.

Can other teams see our cash?

No. Only your team and the host see your cash. Price, quantity and upgrade levels are public. Company Value is shown on the projector, because it's the score everyone is competing on.

Why did we sell nothing even though our price was low?

Price is only part of the score. If rivals had higher Tech or Capacity, or the shock made Price count less, they may have outscored you and used up Apple's demand first. Ask the Analyst for "rivals" next round to see the gap.

Why can't I post in the All Teams channel?

Only your team's leader can. Use the Team channel to tell them what to say, or they can step away and leadership passes to the next connected teammate.

Does the Analyst cheat?

No. It only sees what your team sees on screen, and it can't see rival cash. It predicts what happens if nobody else changes their numbers, which they often will.
